Recent Nature Communications studies identified lymph node metastasis prediction and FTC/PTC subtype classification as two clinically actionable malignant-lesion tasks, because the former can guide surgical extent and adjuvant treatment while the latter affects prognosis and risk stratification; however, both tasks remain difficult in ultrasound due to deep or small lesions, complex anatomy, skip metastasis, severe class imbalance, and only subtle differences in sonographic appearance~\cite{shen2025explainable,dai2025improving}. To assess whether ThyroidXAgent generalises to these clinically actionable malignant-lesion tasks, we further evaluated it on two binary classification problems derived from malignant ultrasound cases, namely lymph node metastasis prediction and FTC/PTC subtype classification. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:ThyroidXAgent_Malignant_Image_tasks}, ThyroidXAgent consistently outperformed the corresponding specialist baselines, and SHAP analyses indicated that morphologic and texture-related features remained the principal drivers of the predictions.
