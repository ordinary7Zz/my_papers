\section{Results}
\subsection{Segmentation, classification performance and interpretability}
We evaluated ThyroidXAgent on five segmentation datasets, four of which also included benign/malignant labels for binary classification, spanning diverse ultrasound devices and acquisition protocols. Across the segmentation benchmarks, ThyroidXAgent achieved a mean Dice of 85.24% and a mean HD95 of 8.30, whereas the UltraFedFM~\cite{jiang2025pretraining} baseline achieved a mean Dice of 78.77% and a mean HD95 of 14.44. For the four datasets with classification labels, the model achieved a mean AUROC of 0.9466 and a mean AUPRC of 0.8361, compared with GPT-5.5~\cite{openai2025gpt5systemcard}, which achieved an AUROC of 0.7025 and an AUPRC of 0.6441 (Fig.~\ref{fig:ThyroidXAgent_SegCls_performance}a). To enable a direct comparison with physicians, we randomly sampled 500 images from the held-out test set for blinded reading by two physicians. On this 500-image evaluation set, ROC and precision--recall analyses showed that ThyroidXAgent achieved the best overall performance among all compared models and physicians, with an AUROC of 0.9256 and an AUPRC of 0.9250 (Fig.~\ref{fig:ThyroidXAgent_SegCls_performance}b).

To examine what underpins these predictions, we analysed cohort-level SHAP values (Fig.~\ref{fig:ThyroidXAgent_SegCls_performance}c)~\cite{lundberg2017unified}. For benign and malignant classification, morphology-related descriptors, particularly Sphericity and Elongation, were the dominant contributors, whereas texture and intensity descriptors provided complementary information. This suggests that ThyroidXAgent primarily exploits clinically meaningful structural cues rather than isolated pixel-level artefacts. In representative cases (Fig.~\ref{fig:ThyroidXAgent_SegCls_performance}d,e), higher-quality segmentation was accompanied by more concentrated attributions along the lesion boundary and more coherent downstream classification, whereas poorer delineation was associated with weaker alignment between image evidence and prediction.

Lymph node metastasis prediction and FTC/PTC subtype classification are clinically important malignant-lesion tasks, yet both remain challenging in ultrasound because of lesion depth or size, complex anatomy, skip metastasis, class imbalance, and subtle sonographic differences~\cite{shen2025explainable,dai2025improving}. We evaluated ThyroidXAgent on these two tasks to test whether it generalises beyond benign–malignant classification and captures morphology-related cues for finer-grained malignant discrimination. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:ThyroidXAgent_Malignant_Image_tasks}a, ThyroidXAgent achieved an AUROC of 0.864 and an AUPRC of 0.881 for lymph node metastasis prediction, corresponding to relative improvements of 12.7% and 19.6% over the LLNM-Net~\cite{shen2025explainable} baseline. For FTC/PTC subtype classification, the model achieved an AUROC of 0.805 and an AUPRC of 0.786, corresponding to relative improvements of 12.9% and 10.5% over the Tiger-Model~\cite{dai2025improving} baseline. SHAP analyses revealed task-specific radiomic signatures: FTC/PTC classification was driven primarily by texture heterogeneity, whereas lymph node metastasis prediction was dominated by intensity- and size-related features, with shape descriptors providing additional signal (Fig.~\ref{fig:ThyroidXAgent_Malignant_Image_tasks}b,c).
